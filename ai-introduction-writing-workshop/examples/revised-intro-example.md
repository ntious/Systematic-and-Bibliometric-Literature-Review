The academic performance of students is one of the integral mechanisms for evaluating an academic institution. It also helps design operative instruments that improve students’ academic outcomes and avoid dropout, among other things. For example, West African second-cycle schools are usually ranked yearly based on the general performance of their students in the West African Examination Council (WAEC). Likewise, in Ghana, the rank of basic educational (primary and junior secondary schools) institutions is partially based on their students’ performance in the Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE). Therefore, a means to correctly articulate students’ academic performance before their final examination at all education levels is critical to academic institutions, policymakers, and educational planners who rely on early indicators for decision-making.

Concerns have been raised in recent years about declining student performance in subjects such as science, mathematics, and information technology. Some nationalists blame the use of foreign languages for teaching as responsible for poor academic performance and even underdevelopment among students [1]. However, other studies [2]–[5] have argued that students’ academic performance extends beyond the language of instruction and fundamentally involves both cognitive characteristics (such as high school grade point average) and non-cognitive characteristics. These non-cognitive characteristics include student engagement, behavioural observations at school, the general views of family and friends concerning schooling, gender, place of birth, and involvement in extracurricular activities. This broader perspective suggests that academic performance is multidimensional and cannot be explained by a single factor.

Advances in technology have resulted in the accumulation of large volumes of educational data containing both cognitive and non-cognitive attributes. Nevertheless, analysing such data using traditional analytical techniques is often challenging [6]. Educational Data Mining (EDM) applies data mining methods to educational datasets in order to uncover meaningful patterns that are not immediately observable. Within EDM, Machine Learning Algorithms (MLAs) provide computational tools for modelling complex relationships between student characteristics and academic outcomes. These techniques enable systematic analysis of performance predictors and support evidence-based interpretation of educational trends.

This paper seeks to examine the degree to which cognitive and non-cognitive characteristics influence the academic performance of students at second-cycle schools using MLAs, as well as to compare and evaluate the predictive performance of different classification methods for predicting students’ academic performance. Specifically, we seek to:
i. Examine which factors, cognitive or non-cognitive or both, are significant predictors of students’ academic performance.
ii. Perform a comparative analysis of different MLAs to identify which algorithm improves prediction accuracy.
iii. Predict the average grade score of a student in mathematics, science, and information technology and estimate whether a student is at risk of failing the final examination based on the best classification algorithm from the comparative analysis.
iv. Examine the effect of k-fold and leave-one-out cross-validation training techniques on the prediction accuracy of different MLAs for predicting students’ academic performance.

The following research questions have been formulated:
RQ1: Which MLAs are appropriate for effective and efficient prediction of students’ academic performance?
RQ2: Which cognitive and non-cognitive factors affect students’ academic performance and the predictive performance of MLAs?

This study contributes by identifying cognitive and non-cognitive factors associated with student performance, evaluating training strategies for predictive modelling, and comparing commonly used classification algorithms within an educational context. Rather than proposing causal interventions, the study focuses on predictive modelling as a tool for understanding patterns that may inform future educational strategies.

This paper uses various machine learning classifiers, including Decision Tree (DT), K-Nearest-Neighbour (KNN), Artificial Neural Network (ANN), Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), AdaBoost, and Support Vector Machine (Radial Basis Function). These techniques were selected based on their established efficiency in classification tasks and prior successful applications in fields such as engineering [7]–[10], finance [11]–[14], and education [15]–[18].
